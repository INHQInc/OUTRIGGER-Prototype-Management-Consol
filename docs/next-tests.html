Outrigger · home page · recommended tests

The next three tests

One hypothesis each. Every change traced to a measurement from the hero experiment, and to Verndale's audit where it supports it.

A

Cheap commitment before expensive one

Property tile rebuild + offer property-picker · run as one bundle

What changes

  • Property tile. Two competing CTAs become one ungated action — “View Property”. “Learn More” is removed; the marketing sentence is replaced by the property's distinctive detail (Monkeypod Kitchen · Duke's + Blue Note · ’Auana by Cirque du Soleil · Appetito), computed from the amenity diff.
  • Offer tiles. A click asks which property first, then hands to the widget with the promo attached — instead of linking straight out to a worldwide list.

Hypothesis

Guests asked to choose a place before a date will reach Rooms & Rates more often, because the page currently demands the expensive commitment first and they decline it.

Supporting metrics — hero experiment

Property Title — ungated link496 → 558+11.3%
Property “Book Now” — opens the date-gated widget294 → 295+1 click / 18 days
Property “Learn More” — the brochure163 → 155−5.9%
Offer CTA — dateless link to a worldwide list198 → 199−0.6%
Rooms & Rates — the step that leaked547 → 526−4.9%

Same card, same guests, two paths: the ungated one wins by an order of magnitude. That single comparison is the whole hypothesis.

What supports it from Verndale

“Book Now may feel too aggressive on the home page… Recommend more scannable CTAs — Explore Property or View Property instead of ‘learn more’.” — annotation 4
“Showcase other examples since Cirque de Soleil ’Auana is only at Waikiki.” — annotation 6

Their instinct on Book Now is confirmed by the number. Annotation 6 is delivered here as the tile's distinctive detail rather than as a new section — which also honours their own principle: “the recommendation is not to add more content.” Audit theme 2, “help travelers choose with confidence,” is the same idea.

B

Soften the widget's date default

Month / flexible instead of an exact-date grid · must run alone

What changes

  • The booking widget opens on month or flexible dates rather than demanding an exact arrival and departure before it will proceed.
  • Nothing else. One component, one default.

Hypothesis

Reducing the precision demanded at the gate will increase progression through it — because every CTA on the page now routes through this one widget, so the gate sits in front of 100% of booking intent.

Supporting metrics — hero experiment

Property “Book Now” — the gate, measured294 → 295+1 click / 18 days
Property Title — same card, no gate496 → 558+11.3%
Destination explorer — intent is being created864 → 946+8.3%
Rooms & Rates — and not converting547 → 526−4.9%

The gate is the only difference between the two paths on the same card. Intent is rising upstream and falling at this step.

What supports it from Verndale

“Consider progressive CTAs: softer CTA on homepage → stronger CTA on property page… add supporting microcopy under CTA to reduce friction.” — annotation 4

They identified the friction and proposed treating it with wording. The measurement says the friction is the date demand itself, so this treats the same problem one layer down.

Why it can't be bundled

It is global — it changes the handler every other CTA passes through. Bundled with A, there would be no way to tell whether a move came from the tile, the offer picker, or the gate. It also has the widest blast radius of the three, so it deserves a clean read.

C

Trip Planner banner

Directly under the destination explorer · must stand alone · one question unresolved

What changes

  • A wide banner sits immediately below the destination explorer, offering entry by vacation type — adventure · wellness · romance · family.
  • It routes into the existing Trip Planner feature.

Hypothesis

Guests who explored but are not ready to book will engage a lower-commitment path rather than leaving — without reducing progression to Rooms & Rates.

Supporting metrics — hero experiment

Destination dropdown — the audience exists375 → 427+12.7%
Destination explorer tabs — and is growing864 → 946+8.3%
Destination Highlights — they refuse the current exit199 → 187−7.0%

What supports it from Verndale

“Consider providing an option for people to search by vacation types rather than location to help people choose — adventure seekers · wellness & spa · romantic getaways · family-friendly.” — annotation 7

This is Homepage 2.0's “Choose Your Journey” section in its cheapest testable form.

The risk, and why it stands alone

Guests take the cheapest available commitment — that is what Title +11.3% against Book Now +1 click means. A save is cheaper than a rates click. Placed directly after the explorer, this may divert people who would otherwise have progressed.

So Rooms & Rates arrivals is the primary and must not fall. Saves rise and arrivals hold → it captured leavers, ship it. Saves rise and arrivals fall → it diverted progressors, move it below the offers. Bundled with A, a win in A would mask exactly this.

Variant that removes the risk instead of measuring it: show it conditionally, after two or more exploration actions with no rates click — targeting leavers directly.

Unresolved and blocking: is this promoting the existing favourites feature, or building a vacation-type chooser? Different builds, different primary metrics. Saving is not booking.

What can share a test, and what can't

Six changes, three tests. The split isn't about how big each change is — it's about whether the result would still mean something afterwards.

1

One hypothesis per test — not one change. Two changes belong together when a single sentence explains why both should work. If you need two sentences, you need two tests.

2

Anything global runs alone. A change to a component every other CTA passes through can't be attributed to any one of them.

3

Anything that could move the primary down runs alone. Bundled with a winner, a diversion is invisible — the bundle nets positive and you ship the thing that costs you bookings.

4

Instrumentation buys you the rest. Changes that fire distinguishable events can share a test, because the funnel tells you which stage moved. That is the difference between bundling and guessing.

Why not run all six at once? You'd get one number and no idea which change produced it — and because C can move the primary the other way, that number could be positive while a change inside it is losing you bookings. The hero experiment is the precedent: it moved several things at once and the useful finding came from the metrics that disagreed with each other, not the headline.

Sequencing. Instrumentation → A (~14 days) → B (~14 days) → C, last because its build depends on an unresolved product question and its risk is best judged once you know what A and B did to the funnel. A and B could run concurrently only if held to disjoint audiences — otherwise B's change is present inside A's arms.

Attribution inside a bundle is recoverable — with instrumentation.

Events at offer → property picked → widget opened → engine reached turn bundle A into a funnel rather than a single number, so you can see which stage moved. That is what makes bundling safe rather than sloppy — and it is already on the required-instrumentation list.

And guardrails catch what attribution misses. If a bundle moves the primary but breaks a guardrail, you know something inside it did harm before you know which part.

Shared measurement frame — all three tests

Primary: Visit Page: Booking Engine: Rooms & Rates, baseline 4.27%, expected to increase. ~14 days for a 20% relative move at 711 visitors/arm/day.

Guardrails: Hero CTA Click must stay down · destination explorer engagement · property tile engagement · offer engagement · scroll depth · bookings per arrival (34.4%).

Watched, not decisive: Booking Complete — at a 1.23% baseline it needs ~8 weeks to resolve and can never be the deciding metric.
