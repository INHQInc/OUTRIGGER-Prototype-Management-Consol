Same width, different divisions

A stage that can never happen shouldn't occupy a slot. Today all five segments render on every card, so a type-3 sits at two dark segments it will never light and reads as permanently behind. That's the board asserting something false.

But dropping to a shorter bar creates a new problem: a 2-of-2 bar and a 2-of-5 bar look nothing alike, and you lose the ability to compare progress down a column. So keep the bar the same total width on every card and divide it by that type's own stages — five thin segments for a measured experiment, four for an injection, three for an Opti-built test. Progress then reads as a proportion, which is comparable across every card, and no card ever shows a stage it cannot reach.

That's the honest version of "how far along is this": far through its own process, not far through the longest process any prototype could have. A type-3 that's testing genuinely is nearly done, and it should look it.

On this example: Regional Map Display is really a type-2 in the current model — it just shipped without ever binding an experiment, which is the type-1 use case being done by hand. It's shown here as the card it would have once the type exists. Whether that fourth segment is "Live" or is followed by a Handoff is open question 1.

The bigger point: nothing asks you

The type is currently a Built where toggle in Settings → Details, found only if you know to look. Creation asks nothing, so everything begins as a full-pipeline console build. That is the same failure you named earlier — "you need to know to go look at a page to even know that" — occurring at step one, and it's worse there than anywhere else, because the wrong answer shapes every gate that follows.

Ask it once, at creation. "What kind of thing is this?" with three plain answers — a change we'll measure with an experiment, a change we just want live, an experiment already built in Optimizely. Answer it once and the console can stop offering steps that will never apply to this prototype. That deletes actions rather than renaming them, which we already agreed beats everything else on the list.

Keep it changeable in Settings, with the warning it has now: switching a type-2 that already has a branch and cut versions over to type-3 orphans real work.

Which columns can a card even go in?

Two different reasons for "no", and the board currently says the same thing to both

Once types exist, some columns aren't merely closed to a card — they're not part of its world. An Opti-built prototype has no Build stage, ever. Today, dragging one at Build bounces with "Build means a real build exists on the branch. It moves when the agent pushes one." — which is not just unhelpful, it's false for that card. It will never have a build, and no amount of waiting will move it there.

So there are three states, not two:

Not in its life. The column is irrelevant to this card. It shouldn't refuse a drop with an explanation — it should visibly not be a place, greyed out for the whole drag the moment you pick the card up.

In its life, but derived. Real stage, but a machine observation puts the card there, not you. Refusing is right; the reason should be stated during the drag, in the column, rather than as a toast after you've already failed.

Yours to declare. The ship, plus reordering inside a column. These are the only two moves a person makes, and they're the ones that should light up.

Show the rule while dragging, not after

This is what actually fixes the complaint you started with. On pick-up, the board already knows the card's type and stage, so it can paint the answer immediately: legal targets accent-lit, derived columns plain with a one-line reason in place, out-of-life columns greyed to about a third. You learn the rule by looking instead of by failing, and the toast drops back to being a fallback rather than the primary teacher.

It also means the honest count is small and knowable: for any card there is at most one legal cross-column move, and often none. That's worth showing plainly rather than leaving someone to discover it by dragging at five columns in turn.

Note the two open questions land right here. Until Q1 and Q3 are answered, the Done column for types 1 and 3 is neither "yours" nor "not in its life" — which is exactly why a type-3 ship is currently accepted and then silently reverted.

What I'd do

A recommendation, not a menu

A for the frame, B for the body. Lead with who it's waiting on and for how long — that's the question the board exists to answer, and it's the line that makes it stop feeling like a build system. Then let the card body change with the stage: work that's waiting shows the blocker and one button (A); work that's live or finished shows the claim and how it's doing (B). A card for a running experiment shouldn't be shaped like a task, because it isn't one.

C's contribution is the aging — "stuck in Build 6 days" — which I'd take into A's status line rather than building a third card. One card grammar per room still holds; the body varies by stage, the frame doesn't.

None of this touches a gate. The derivation, the first-gate hold, the running-experiment lock and the refusal to start traffic all stay exactly as they are — which is what keeps the "don't let them mess themselves up" half intact while the tool stops sounding like a terminal.

On the numbers shown: owner reads "Unassigned" because the model has an owner field that nothing populates and the card never renders. Elapsed times ("6 days", "4 days") are illustrative of the shape — they're derivable from updatedAt, which the card doesn't currently read. Everything else — names, descriptions, metrics, guardrail counts, version numbers, live states — is your real data as of this build.
