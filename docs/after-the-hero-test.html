Outrigger · post-experiment brief

The hero worked. The handoff didn't.

Home Page Hero No Offer — what 17 days and 10,791 guests per arm actually established, and the test that should follow it.

The headline you may not expect

The readout calls the booking-engine dip "what it cost". The counts say something else

The most important number isn't on the dashboard

Fewer guests reached Rooms & Rates — and more of them booked.

Booking-engine visits fell from 436 → 414. Completed bookings rose from 117 → 142. If those sit on the same path, the arrival-to-booking rate went from 26.8% → 34.3% — roughly a 28% relative improvement in how well the booking engine converts. The funnel didn't spring a leak. It got narrower and better qualified.

Stated as a signal, not a finding. Neither underlying number is significant on its own (p=0.328 and p=0.147), and a ratio of two noisy counts is noisier still. It also assumes bookings arrive via that page — Property Book Now CTA is a second entry point and rose slightly too. Confirm the path before you build on this.

4 · Removing the offer did not reduce offer-seeking

Offer CTA +9.8%, See-all-offers +33.8%, offer engagement composite +11.8%. Guests who want a deal still find one — slightly more readily. That closes off the obvious commercial objection to shipping this hero.

The verdict to stamp

Adjudicated against the frozen pre-registration, honestly

Partial — mechanism confirmed, outcome unproven

The brief claimed two things. Fewer hero offer clicks: settled, decisively. More downstream exploration lifting bookings: the direction is right on every exploration metric and on bookings, but nothing crossed significance in 17 days, and the booking-engine step moved the wrong way. Stamp it as confirmed on the primary, not adjudicable on the outcome — not as a win.

Recommendation — ship the hero anyway

No guardrail breached. Scroll behaviour unharmed, offer-seeking unharmed, bookings leaning positive, and the strategic goal — stop leading with a discount — is achieved outright. The one negative, booking-engine visits, is unproven and plausibly a qualification effect rather than a loss. Ship it, and make it the new control. The follow-up must run on top of the new hero, or you'll spend another 17 days re-learning what you already know.

The follow-up brief

Ready to freeze as a pre-registration

The gap is specific and the data points straight at it. Guests explore destinations more (+9.1%), open the dropdown more (+10.6%) and click into properties more (+7.4%) — then reach Rooms & Rates less (−6.4%). Exploration intent is being manufactured successfully and then dropped on the floor. The next test is the bridge from exploring a place to shopping a room — not the hero, and not more home page content.

Home Page Hero — Explore to Rooms

Hypothesis

We believe that giving the destination explorer and property tiles a direct “See rooms & rates” entry — carrying the guest into room selection rather than into another browse page — for home page visitors who engage with exploration on the new experience-led hero, will cause more arrivals at the Rooms & Rates booking engine, because the intent the new hero creates currently has no short path to a room, which in turn should carry through to bookings.

Primary metric · expected to INCREASE

Visit Page: Booking Engine: Rooms & Rates — baseline 4.04%. It is the one step that moved the wrong way, and the only step between proven intent and revenue.

Guardrails — protect what already works

  • Hero CTA Click — must stay down; if it rebounds the new hero has been undone
  • Destination explorer engagement — the composite that gained; must not fall
  • Property tile engagement — the other gainer
  • Offer engagement — must not fall; it rose last time
  • Scroll depth engagement — flat is fine; a drop means we've buried something

Directional outcome — NOT the decision metric

Booking Complete (baseline 1.08%). At this traffic, detecting a 20% move on a 1.08% metric needs roughly 36,000 per arm — about eight weeks. Pre-registering it as primary would guarantee an unresolvable test. Watch it; don't bet the decision on it.

Power and duration — computed, not guessed

This run achieved 10,791/arm in 17 days (~635/arm/day) and could resolve ±20.5% on a ~3.7% metric. The new primary sits at 4.04%, so 17–21 days detects roughly a 20% relative move. A 10% move would need ~38,000/arm — about nine weeks. Pre-commit to detecting 20% and accept that smaller effects won't resolve.

Audience

Home page visitors, new hero shown to all. If the sample allows, pre-register first-time visitors as the segment of interest — the original brief named them, and they're the guests with no prior brand context to fall back on.

What to build — three candidates, ranked by what the data supports

  1. Rooms-first destination explorer

    The explorer tab is the single biggest gainer (+9.1%, +70 clicks). Whatever it currently leads to, it isn't rooms. Give each destination result a direct rooms-and-dates entry so the click after exploration is a shopping click. Highest-traffic surface, most direct line to the primary metric.

  2. Property tile goes to rooms, not to a landing page

    Property Title clicks rose +7.4% while Property Learn More fell −9.6% — guests want the place, not more prose about it. Point the tile's primary action at Rooms & Rates for that property. Small change, and the metrics already argue for it.

  3. Carry the intent forward

    After two or more exploration actions, surface a lightweight dates-and-occupancy prompt so the booking engine is reached pre-qualified. Most speculative of the three and the most build; hold it unless the first two under-deliver.

One thing to settle first

It decides whether the follow-up's primary metric is the right one

Data hygiene

Confirm bookings actually pass through that page.

The whole "narrower but better-qualified funnel" reading rests on Booking Complete sitting downstream of Visit Page: Booking Engine: Rooms & Rates. If a material share of bookings enters another way — Property Book Now CTA rose slightly — then the 26.8% → 34.3% figure is comparing two different populations and means much less. One analytics check settles it, and it decides whether the follow-up's primary metric is the right one.

The sequence

In order

  1. Stop it in Optimizely, then close it out in Prism

    Ending the run raises the adjudication prompt; stamping the verdict against the frozen pre-registration is what makes this a record rather than a memory. Stamp it partial.

  2. Ship the new hero as the permanent control

    No guardrail breached and the strategic goal is met. Everything downstream now assumes it.

  3. Confirm the booking path

    Whether Booking Complete actually sits downstream of Rooms & Rates. One analytics check, and it decides whether the follow-up's primary metric is the right one.

  4. Freeze the follow-up brief and build candidate 1

    Rooms-first destination explorer. Pre-register at 20% detectable, 17–21 days, with Booking Complete watched but not decisive.
