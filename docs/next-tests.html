Outrigger home page · what we test next

Four tests, in order

One idea each. Every one of them comes straight out of a number the last test already gave us.

01

Nothing on the property card asks for a date

Change

Book Now becomes View Rooms. Learn More goes away — the property name is already a link and already does that job. Offer clicks ask which hotel first, then dates.

Why

Right now the card asks for the hardest commitment first, and people say no.

Proof
+11.3%the property name — no date asked
+1 clickBook Now — in 18 days
−5.9%Learn More

Same card, same people. The control that asks for nothing gained 11%. The one that demands dates gained a single click in eighteen days — it isn't a booking path, it's decoration. And "Learn More" loses because it promises nothing; "View Rooms" names what you get. Verndale said the same: Book Now is too aggressive here.

02

Make picking dates one tap instead of four

Change

The booking widget still asks for a start and end date — but we fill them in for you, or give you one-tap choices, instead of an empty calendar.

Why

Every single button on the page now goes through that one widget.

Proof
+8.3%more people exploring destinations
−4.9%fewer reaching rates

We're creating more interest and losing it at the same step. The widget sits in front of one hundred percent of it.

03

A Trip Planner banner under the destination explorer

Change

A wide banner: pick your kind of trip — adventure, wellness, romance, family — into the Trip Planner we already have.

Why

Catch the people who are browsing and aren't ready to book yet.

Proof
+12.7%using the destination dropdown
−7.0%taking the exit we give them today

The one to watch. People take the easiest option in front of them — and saving a trip is easier than booking one. So rates traffic can't drop. If it does, the banner moves further down the page. This is Verndale's vacation-type idea.

04

The destination name becomes the way you change destination

Change

The dropdown goes. Hawaii stays exactly where it is, at full size — and the other four destinations sit next to it as pills. Pick one and it becomes the headline. Nothing is hidden.

Why

Our best control is also the one people can’t find.

Proof
+12.7%the dropdown — biggest gain on the page
+8.3%destination tabs

Verndale’s reviewer said it plainly: “I also missed the drop down at first.” And the numbers say that missed control is the strongest performer we have. So this isn’t tidying up a duplicate — it’s taking the best thing on the page out from behind a menu.

Why pills and not something cleverer. A big word with a small chevron looks better and is harder to find than the dropdown it replaces — which is the exact problem we’re fixing. Pills look like buttons, so the whole set is visible without clicking. Explorer engagement can’t fall; that’s the test.

Why four tests and not one

If we change everything at once, we get one number and no idea what caused it.

The card and the offer go together.
Same idea, and we can tag the clicks separately to see which one moved.
The widget goes alone.
It's behind every other button — bundled in, we couldn't tell what worked.
The banner goes alone.
It's the only one that could make things worse. Bundled with a winner, we'd never see it — and we'd ship it.
The destination selector goes alone.
It changes the best-performing part of the page. If it costs us anything, we need to see that on its own, not buried under someone else's win.

Order: tagging first, then 01, 02, 03, 04. Two weeks each. We're measuring one thing: people reaching Rooms & Rates.
