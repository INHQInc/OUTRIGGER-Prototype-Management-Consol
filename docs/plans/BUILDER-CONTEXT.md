## 0. The two facts that frame everything

**Onboarding today captures nothing about the customer or the site as a subject.** The whole of it is five plumbing steps at `src/app/page.tsx:61-66`: add an environment URL, connect GitHub, register a repo, connect Optimizely, install the loader tag. The records those produce are `Org {id, name, createdAt}` (`src/lib/orgs.ts:10-14`) and `Environment {id, orgId, siteKey?, label, url, kind, createdAt}` (`src/lib/environments.ts:15-26`). A customer can be fully onboarded and the system will not know what business they are in, who visits, what they call their visitors, or what their site is built out of.

**Capture without a delivery seam changes nothing.** `src/lib/prototypes/provision.ts:8-24` — the import block — contains no import from `../brand`. I verified this directly. The entire three-layer context model reaches exactly one consumer, `src/lib/ai/results.ts:28,58,59`, the readout. And skills are written to the branch as literal bytes (`provision.ts:332` writes `sk.body` verbatim), so there is no template pass through which a captured field could ever replace a hardcoded string in a skill. **Any field added by onboarding needs a file on the branch to land in and a hash to travel under. That plumbing is a precondition for all three tiers, not a phase-two nicety.**

A trap worth stating once, because it will be missed: `contentHashOf()` (`provision.ts:162-173`) hashes only name, brief, hypothesis, metrics, target URLs and attachment names. Site- or customer-level context added to the payload and **not** added to that hash means a branch never learns the context changed — the console will report it as current forever.

## 3. THE MINIMUM VIABLE CUT

**Build the CUSTOMER tier first — but the first commit is the delivery seam, and it carries three already-captured site facts for free.**

### The cut

**Step 0 — the seam (a precondition, not a tier).** Three changes, none of which capture anything:
1. `provision.ts` imports from `../brand` and writes a new `.opmc/customer.md` (characterized prose — voice, audience, vertical, standing guardrails) plus a `context.json.site` block (structured data).
2. Those inputs join `contentHashOf()` (`provision.ts:162-173`) — otherwise the branch never learns they changed.
3. A root `CLAUDE.md` is provisioned (`HANDOFF.md:796`, already scoped), so orientation stops depending on the human pasting the right launch string.

**Step 1 — five customer fields.**

**Step 2 — the free riders.** Three facts the console already computes and stores, delivered in the same payload with zero capture work: the `CompatReport` for the target environment (`compat.ts:61-88` → flag `compat:<envId>`), `TargetInjection` per page (`types.ts:57-63`, currently dropped at `provision.ts:289`), and the certification thresholds as numbers (`certify.ts:35-36`). Plus `ExperimentationConfig.provider` (`experimentation/types.ts:26`), which already exists and un-hardcodes the timing rule's vendor name.

### Why customer, not site

**Frequency.** Every prototype ships copy. Voice, vertical and taxonomy bite on 100% of builds. The site-tier items with the largest individual payoff — source layout, breakpoints, instrumentation convention — bite on a subset (handoff time; sites whose breakpoints differ from 1280/768/375; metrics that need a new hook).

**Cost.** The taxonomy half is *finished*: `Taxonomy`, `requireTaxonomy`, `taxonomyPrompt` (`profile.ts:117-159,220-228`), seeded for the live customer. Voice and audience are declared keys awaiting a writer. The vertical is one string. Against that, the site tier's marquee items need ingestion machinery that does not exist — `CONTEXT-INGESTION.md:99-107` names breadth (map + bounded crawl, with an unpriced cost ceiling), durability and staleness as unbuilt, and §6 at `:225-229` adds source ingestion on top. "Site tier first" is really "build a crawler and a repo reader first", and nothing reaches the builder for weeks.

**It unblocks other work.** `CONTEXT-INGESTION.md:234-235` states plainly that three `results.ts` strings cannot be converted without vertical/audience/voice. The customer tier is the only one on the critical path of work already in flight.

**It is the failure a reviewer sees.** An off-brand headline gets the idea rejected. A 768px-vs-834px breakpoint miss gets caught in QA. Both are real, but only one of them loses the experiment before it runs.

### What I am deliberately *not* putting in the first cut, and why

- **The earned digest.** `earnedDigest()` (`profile.ts:242-264`) has zero callers *and* `addBrandFact` has zero callers outside the two store implementations — I verified both. So it is a two-part build: a writer at verdict-stamping time, then a reader. Its own docstring (`profile.ts:232-238`) says it should be the loudest thing in the builder's context, and I agree it is the highest-ceiling item in the whole document. But it produces nothing on day one because there are no facts yet. **Start writing `BrandFact`s in the same release; deliver the digest in the next.**
- **Source layout.** Highest per-incident value, but it has exactly one customer today and that customer's stack is correct in all three hardcoded copies. It becomes urgent at customer number two, not before. It should nonetheless be the *next* thing, because `handoff/resolve.ts:14` hardcodes an absolute filesystem path and will fail silently rather than loudly.

### The honest counter-argument

Site tier first is defensible on one ground: four of its items cost nearly nothing because the data is already captured (S7 CSP, S8 bot, S11 rendering model, and the dropped `TargetInjection`). That is why they are in **Step 2** of this cut rather than in a later tier. The cut above takes them; it just does not pretend they constitute a site tier.

## 5. DISAGREEMENTS AND OPEN QUESTIONS

### Disagreements between readers — adjudicated

**1. Which tier owns the EARNED layer.** The brief reader filed it as *site*; the provision and skills readers filed it as *customer*. **The schema settles it: site.** `BrandFact` carries both `orgId` *and* `siteId` (`brand/types.ts:175-176`), and `SiteProfile`'s own comment states *"the unit of characterization is the SITE, not the customer, because a crawl is of a site and two sites of one customer do not share a voice"* (`types.ts:140-143`). Resolution inherits customer→site, exactly as taxonomy does.

**2. Which tier owns MEASUREMENT.** The brief reader filed it *prototype*; the provision and measurement readers filed it *site*. **Both, split cleanly:** the *convention* — the tracking-hook attribute, its value vocabulary, which elements already fire what — is SITE (S3, S4). *Which* event decides *this* experiment is PROTOTYPE (P2). Building only one half leaves the builder unable to act.

**3. Which tier owns SOURCE LAYOUT.** Filed *site* by most, but `OrgRepo` is **org**-scoped (`git/types.ts:65-75`) while the layout describes a site's codebase. `CONTEXT-INGESTION.md:160-165` raises exactly this and proposes the answer: keep `OrgRepo` as the registry, and have the *site profile reference* one or more source repos plus a subtree path rather than own them. **Adopt that.** It survives a monorepo serving several sites and a site assembled from several repos.

**4. Site-wide vs per-environment.** No reader separated these, and it matters. CSP, WAF/auth posture, loader presence, and the Optimizely project id differ **per environment** (prep vs prod) — they belong on `Environment`. Source layout, breakpoints, vocabulary, vendor inventory and instrumentation convention are **site-wide** — they belong on `SiteProfile`. Putting CSP on the site profile will be wrong the first time prep and prod differ, which is the normal case.

### Open questions a human must settle

**Q1 — BLOCKING. May a variation fire an event, and how?** `certify.ts:39-45,87-90` FAILs the push on `dataLayer.push(`, `gtag(`, `_satellite`, `adobeDataLayer`, and `builtins.ts:227` makes "zero analytics" a definition-of-done line. `MeasurementPanel.tsx:122` asks the builder to instrument. On a site whose convention is `dataLayer.push({event:…})` — which is what `data-tag-item` hooks feed — instrumenting correctly makes certification fail. **Nothing else in the measurement chain can be designed until this is answered.** The likely shape: the sanctioned mechanism is copying the site's tracking hook onto the new element (which is what all three shipped prototypes actually do), and the certify rule means "no *new* analytics vendors", not "no measurability" — but that is a product call, and the check currently does not distinguish them.

**Q2 — Is source required to onboard a site?** `CONTEXT-INGESTION.md:170-171` marks this **NOT YET DECIDED** and frames it correctly: either source is genuinely required, or a profile may be approved crawl-only and marked as lacking its source half, with the builder *refusing* rather than silently building worse. The `requireTaxonomy` precedent (`profile.ts:86-116`) argues for refusing. Repo access on day one is a real commercial barrier.

**Q3 — Does the Site tier come back as an entity?** `AGENTS.md:32` says there is no Site entity; `CONTEXT-INGESTION.md:76-87` requires one and `skills.ts:22` has no site scope (`"global" | "brand" | "prototype"`). My recommendation in §1B is to keep three nouns and key `SiteProfile` on the environment id, as `brand/types.ts:138-144` already anticipates — but note this decision also determines whether `SkillScope` gains a `siteId`, which `CONTEXT-INGESTION.md:86-88` says it must.

**Q4 — Who writes `BrandFact`s, and when?** `addBrandFact` has zero callers. The obvious hook is verdict stamping, but that means deciding which verdicts produce which `kind`, and what `expiresAt` to set (the type says *"Baselines decay faster than wins"*, `types.ts:193-196`, without saying how much faster).

**Q5 — Vertical: free text, or an enum?** Free text is honest and cheap and reaches the prompt intact. An enum could drive a pattern library later. Choosing free text now and an enum later means a migration; choosing an enum now means guessing the list.

**Q6 — Does a provisioned `CLAUDE.md` replace `.opmc/`, or point at it?** `HANDOFF.md:796` lists it as open hardening. Pointing is the smaller change; replacing would let the wake-up directive carry the resolved customer context directly, which is exactly what Claude Code auto-loads.

**Q7 — Who approves the characterized layer, and where?** `CONTEXT-INGESTION.md:108-110` calls it *"a UX moment, not a background job"*. Nothing in the console has that room today, and `SiteProfile.status: "draft"|"approved"|"superseded"` plus `corrections[]` (`types.ts:146,160`) is the schema waiting for it.

## 6. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

The building agent today receives an excellent picture of **one page** and **one brief**, and nothing at all about the **site** or the **customer**. Everything genuinely site-specific in its payload is re-derived at provision time from a snapshot of the one-to-three pages this prototype targets; everything customer-specific is a constant baked into a global skill. Customer onboarding must start capturing vertical, audience, voice, standing guardrails and the experimentation platform; site onboarding must capture source layout, instrumentation convention, breakpoints, vendor inventory, locale, asset hosts and per-environment posture; prototype setup must capture a validated anchor, a measurable metric contract, arm and lineage context, device scope and an explicit environment binding. But the first commit is none of those — it is the delivery seam, because `provision.ts:8-24` imports nothing from `../brand` and `provision.ts:332` writes skill bodies verbatim, so a captured field has no path to the branch and no template slot to fill. Build the seam, hand over the three facts the console already measured and threw away, then ship five customer fields. The rest follows a decision about whether a variation may fire an event.
