Outrigger · home page · destination explorer

Three views, one exit

Evolving the destination selector into a real exploration tool — list, grid and map — and the single rule that decides whether it earns anything.

You've picked the right surface

It's the best-performing thing on the page and the worst-converting

The destination explorer is where v1's redirected attention actually went. Destination tabs +8.3% (+82 clicks), the dropdown +12.7% (+52). Those are the two biggest relative gains on the page.

And its own exit button — Home: Destination Highlights Button Click — is −7.0%. More guests are opening the explorer and fewer are taking what it offers them. It is simultaneously the most attractive surface on the page and a dead end. Rebuilding it is well-aimed.

The rule that decides this: every view must exit into a price, never into a page.

v1's whole lesson in one line. We successfully moved 286 clicks out of the offer and into browsing, and gained one click on Book Now, one on the offer CTA, and lost 21 on Rooms & Rates. A richer explorer that still exits into a destination page or a property landing page will reproduce that result at greater expense — more beautiful browsing, the same flat commerce.

Worth flagging directly: your Regional Map Display prototype is measured on “clicks on the ‘Visit property’ link from a map overlay”. That is a click into a page. If the map view inherits that exit, it inherits the outcome.

Three views are three questions

Not three skins on the same data

The failure mode of a view switcher is that all three render the same cards in different shapes, which gives the guest a choice they have no reason to care about. Each view earns its place only if it answers a question the others answer badly.

Grid — “which of these looks like my trip?”

Grid view · the inspiration lens

Image-first. The default, and the closest thing to what exists today.

What it shows

  • Large photography, one line of character — not a paragraph of description
  • A from-rate for the guest's month, and an availability signal
  • Two or three attribute chips: reef swimming · walk to Duke's · adults-only quiet

Why it exists

Guests who don't know Hawai'i choose emotionally, from an image. This is the view that lets them.

Exit → See rooms & rates for that property, on the captured dates.

List — “how do these actually differ?”

List view · the comparison lens

The view the current page has no equivalent of, and the one the data most argues for.

What it shows

  • A real comparison row per property: from-rate, beach type, walk time to the sand, pool, dining, ideal-for
  • Sortable — by price, by proximity to the beach, by size
  • Cross-island by default, so “Oahu or Maui?” is answerable in one screen

Why it exists

Property titles rose +11.3% while Learn More fell −5.9% and Book Now stayed flat. Guests are opening properties to compare them, and the page gives them nothing to compare on — so they open several, learn nothing decisive, and leave. This view is the direct answer to that behaviour.

Exit → See rooms & rates, or add to a shortlist that carries into the engine.

Map — “where is this in relation to everything else?”

Map view · the geography lens

Absorbs the Regional Map Display prototype — with its exit changed.

What it shows

  • Islands and properties in real relation to one another — the thing a grid genuinely cannot express
  • Proximity to what guests actually ask about: the beach, the airport, Waikīkī, the volcano
  • Multi-property trips — the only view that can make “three nights O'ahu, four on Maui” legible

Why it exists

First-time visitors to Hawai'i frequently do not know the islands are separate flights. The map is the only view that answers a question they don't know they have.

Exit → Pin opens rooms & rates for that property, not a “Visit property” page link.

What the three must share

Or it's three tools, not one

One result set and one filter state. Switching view must never lose context. If a guest has narrowed to “O'ahu, March, near the beach”, all three render that narrowing. A view switch is a change of lens, never a restart.

One dated context. This is where the explorer meets the Plan Bar. Every rate shown in every view is a rate for their month. Without that, all three views degrade to a brochure with three layouts — which is precisely today's problem with more engineering behind it.

One exit verb. “See rates” in all three. Not Book Now in grid, Learn More in list and Visit property on the map. One card grammar, one action, so the guest learns the tool once.

The one thing that would make this fail.

Building all three views beautifully and leaving the exits as they are. The explorer would then do what it does today, only better: attract more attention and convert none of it. If you ship nothing else from this spec, ship the exit change — every view, every card, every pin, into a dated rate. That single decision is worth more than the three views combined, and it is testable on the existing explorer before any of this is built.

What I'd sequence

Change the current explorer's exit from Oahu highlights to a dated rates entry, and run that alone. It is a small change on the highest-traffic surface, it isolates the mechanism, and it tells you whether the three-view build is worth funding before you fund it. Then build the views on a proven exit.
