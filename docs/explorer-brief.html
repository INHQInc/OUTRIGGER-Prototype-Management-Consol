The Dated Explorer

The brief for turning the destination selector into a list, grid and map exploration tool where every view carries the guest's dates into the booking engine.

Pre-flight answered, and it changes the shape of this brief.

I loaded reservation.outrigger.com/?chain=18497&arrive=2026-10-02&depart=2026-10-05&adult=2 and the engine honours the dates — Check-in Fri Oct 2, Check-out Mon Oct 5, 2 adults — and lands directly on results. The canonical parameter set is arrive · depart · adult · child · rooms · chain · dest · level · locale. Carrying dates into the engine is a front-end change. Nothing needs building server-side.

And the page it lands on is not a form. It is “Select a Hotel”: a property grid with photography, location, a from-rate per night (“From $210”, “From $567”), a sort control, a “Details” link, a VIEW RATES button on every card — and social proof (“Booked in last 27 minutes”).

Which means the tool you want to build largely exists — behind a date wall.

Outrigger already has a rated, sorted, cross-destination property comparison view. It is called Select a Hotel, it lives in SynXis, and the only way to reach it is to already know your dates. The home page sends people to browse; the comparison tool sits one wall away; and the wall is a date field.

That is a much better problem than the one this brief opened with. It reframes the home page's job: not to rebuild comparison, but to manufacture the dates that unlock it — and to supply the one thing the engine genuinely cannot: geography, and a reason to care.

So challenge your own scope before you fund it.

The list view may be redundant. The engine's grid already does rated comparison, sortable, across every destination — better than a marketing page can without a rate feed. The grid view is close to what the home page already shows. The map view is the one the engine cannot do: SynXis has no geography, and “which island, and how far apart are they” is the question first-time Hawai'i visitors most need answered.

My honest read: build the date capture and the map. Let the engine be the list. If the first test proves dates are the barrier, you will have bought the comparison view for free.

The original correction, which still stands.

My first draft put a from-rate on every card. You're right that the site has no rates — they only exist in the booking engine. But the home page's own source says something more useful than that.

All three Sabre SynXis widget bundles are already loading on outrigger.com: shs-widgets-calendar, shs-widgets-searchbar, and shs-widgets-best-price. The calendar is instantiated — <sabre-shs-widgets-calendar api=…> is in the markup. The search bar and the best-price widget are loaded and never used.

So a date capture is a supported component, not a custom build. And a rate on a card may be one component away rather than one integration away — subject to the pre-flight below, since I am inferring what best-price renders from its name, not from having seen it run.

Which reframes the whole test — and probably explains the flat Book Now.

The home page already deep-links into SynXis with real parameters: chain, dest, nights, promo, filter, adult, level. Every offer tile does it. What none of them carry is a date.

That is very likely why property “Book Now” gained exactly one click in eighteen days. It does reach the booking engine — and lands the guest on an empty search form. It is a door into a waiting room.

So the rule is not “put a price on the card”. It is: carry the guest's dates into the engine so arriving means seeing rooms, not filling in a form. That is buildable today with parameters the site already uses.

Name

Home Page Destination Explorer — List, Grid, Map

The change

Capture an island and a rough month once on the home page, and replace the destination tabs with a persistent exploration tool offering three views of the same result set — grid, list and map — where every card, row and pin opens the booking engine with that destination and those dates already applied, rather than opening a landing page or an empty search form.

Hypothesis

We believe that capturing a destination and rough dates on the home page, and turning the destination selector into a real exploration tool whose every exit carries that context into the booking engine, for home page visitors on the experience-led hero — especially first-time visitors with no prior sense of the islands — will cause more arrivals at the Rooms & Rates booking engine, because the new hero already sends materially more guests into the explorer, the explorer currently offers them nothing but more reading, and the one route that does reach the engine drops them on an empty search form.

Audience

All home page visitors, on the experience-led hero as the new control. Pre-register first-time visitors as the segment of interest — the original hero brief named them, and they are the guests who cannot tell one property from another.

Primary metric · expected to INCREASE

Visit Page: Booking Engine: Rooms & Rates — baseline 4.27%. The only step between proven intent and revenue, and the one measure v1 moved the wrong way.

Guardrails — what must not fall back

  • Hero CTA Click — must stay down. A rebound means we reopened the old door.
  • Destination explorer engagement — v1's biggest gain (+7.3% composite)
  • Property tile engagement — the other gain (+4.7% composite)
  • Offer engagement — flat in v1; a fall means the page turned transactional
  • Scroll depth engagement — already drifting down; must not accelerate
  • Bookings per Rooms & Rates arrival — 34.4% in v1. Volume must not cost quality.

The anti-goal — the measure that catches the failure mode

Explorer engagements per Rooms & Rates arrival must not increase. If it rises, the tool attracted more attention and converted none of it — which is exactly what v1 did, and the reason this metric exists. This is a stop condition, not a nice-to-have.

Watched, not decisive

Booking Complete (baseline 1.23%). At this traffic, resolving a twenty percent move needs roughly 36,000 per arm — about eight weeks. Pre-registering it as primary would guarantee an unresolvable test.

Power and duration — computed, not guessed

v1 accrued 12,804 per arm in 18 days (~711/arm/day) and resolved ±18.2%. At a 4.27% baseline the primary needs about 9,600 per arm — roughly 14 days for a twenty percent relative move. A ten percent move would need ~38,000 per arm, about eight weeks. Pre-commit to detecting twenty percent.

Target page

outrigger.com — home page only. No change below the fold.

What gets built

One result set, three lenses, one exit verb

ViewThe question it answersWhat each card carries
Grid
default		 “Which of these looks like my trip?” Photography, one line of character, two or three attribute chips — plus a rate, if the pre-flight clears
List “How do these actually differ?” Comparison row — beach type, walk to sand, pool, dining, ideal-for. Sortable, cross-island. Rate column if the pre-flight clears.
Map “Where is this relative to everything else?” Real island geography, proximity to beach/airport/Waikīkī, multi-property trips

Shared across all three: one result set and one filter state (a view switch is a change of lens, never a restart); one captured context — island and month — carried into every exit URL; and one exit verb, “See rates”, everywhere. Not Book Now in grid, Learn More in list and Visit property on the map.

The exit is a SynXis deep link built from the guest's own answers — reservation.outrigger.com/?chain=18497&dest=…&hotel=…&arrive=…&depart=… — so the booking engine opens on rooms rather than on a form. The site already builds these links for every offer tile; it has simply never put a date in one.

The exit rule is the test. Everything else is the packaging.

v1 moved 286 clicks into browsing and gained one click on Book Now, one on the offer CTA, and lost 21 on Rooms & Rates. A richer explorer that still exits into a page — or into an undated search form — will reproduce that at greater expense.

Your Regional Map Display prototype is currently measured on “clicks on the ‘Visit property’ link” — a click into a page. If the map view inherits that exit, it inherits the outcome.

What has to be instrumented first

The brief cannot be adjudicated without these

The current event set can tell us the explorer was used. It cannot tell us which view earned it, and that is most of what we would want to learn. Before the start gate:

  • View switch — grid / list / map, so we can attribute the effect to a lens rather than to the tool
  • Filter and sort applied — which axes guests actually use, which is the input to the next iteration
  • Explorer → rates exit, tagged with the originating view — distinct from today's Home: Destination Highlights Button Click and from the map prototype's “Visit property” link
  • Shortlist add, if the list view ships with one

Without the first and third, the anti-goal ratio can't be computed, and the anti-goal is the reason this brief is safe to run.

Pre-flight — three things to confirm before the build is scoped.

1. Does reservation.outrigger.com honour arrival and departure parameters? ANSWERED — yes. Verified live: dates, guests and room count all apply, and the engine lands on the rated hotel grid. This brief is a front-end change.

1b. How do we filter the engine to one island? dest=ORH did not filter — the results came back chain-wide (Maldives, Thailand, Fiji alongside Waikīkī) and the URL normalised to level=chain. An island-scoped exit needs the correct region code or level=hotel&hotel=<id>. This is now the one unknown that actually blocks the build.

2. What does shs-widgets-best-price actually render? Its script is already on the home page and nothing instantiates it. The engine clearly holds per-property from-rates for a date range — it prints them on every card — so the widget almost certainly surfaces the same figure. Still worth confirming, but the brief no longer depends on it: the dated exit carries the hypothesis on its own.

3. Is shs-widgets-searchbar the right Plan Bar? Also loaded, also unused. Using Sabre's own component beats building one, provided it can be styled to the brand.

One data check before the start gate.

Confirm that Booking Complete actually sits downstream of Visit Page: Booking Engine: Rooms & Rates. The home page has at least four other routes into booking — header, sticky bar, tile Book Now, and five offer “Check availability” buttons. If a material share of bookings enters another way, the primary metric measures only part of the funnel and the 34.4% quality guardrail compares two different populations.

Explicitly out of scope

  • Anything below the fold. Scroll is flat to negative at all four depths; new content there would be served to the same eyeballs.
  • The hero. −58.1% at p<0.0001 is settled. Changing it again in the same test would confound the explorer's effect.
  • Offer-led framing. Offer engagement was flat when the offer left the hero — demand for it is inelastic to the home page.
  • Room-attribute taxonomy. Choosing by room type rather than property is a bigger, separate idea; it should not ride along and dilute this reading.
  • Building a rate feed. If the best-price widget doesn't do it, we do without. The dated exit is the hypothesis; rates on cards are an amplifier.

The smaller test worth considering instead

Add the date capture and change only the current explorer's exit — from Oahu highlights to a dated SynXis deep link — and run that alone for two weeks. No new views, no new content. It isolates the exact mechanism this whole brief rests on, on the highest-traffic surface, for a fraction of the build.

If it moves the primary, the three-view build is funded by evidence. If it doesn't, the exit rule is wrong and better views would not have saved it. Given the tile Book Now result — one click in eighteen days into an undated form — this is the cheapest way to find out whether dates are the barrier.
