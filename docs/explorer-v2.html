Why the map opens at world scale

Projecting all sixteen properties, the four Waikīkī hotels land on the same pixel — they sit within 800 metres of each other. At estate scale the map isn't showing properties, it's showing five destinations across three oceans. That's the question a tab strip can't answer: Hawai‘i, or Thailand, or Fiji?

Navigating the hierarchy

One row that drills — because the estate isn't a uniform depth

The problem

Hawai‘i has four regions beneath it. Thailand, Fiji, Mauritius and Maldives have none — properties hang straight off the destination. A fixed two-row tab strip shows an empty second row four times out of five, and privileges Hawai‘i structurally for no reason a guest would understand.

The rule

The row always shows the children of wherever you are. If the current node has regions, the row shows regions. If it has properties, the row shows nothing more — the list already has them. One rule, both depths, never an empty row.

Three things this gets right

  • Zooming the map is navigating. The row and the map are one state. Click the Hawai‘i cluster and the row drills; drill the row and the map flies. Neither is a separate control to learn.
  • Counts do real work. “Mauritius 1” tells you not to bother drilling. “Hawai‘i 8” tells you there's something to explore. The asymmetry stops being a design problem and becomes information.
  • The dropdown survives as a jump. Your biggest gainer (+12.7%) is people going straight to a destination. Drilling is for browsers; keep a “Jump to…” select for people who already know. Same state, second door.

The one I'd watch

“All 8” vs picking an island. At Hawai‘i level the list shows all eight across four islands, which is a genuinely useful comparison — but it's also the moment a guest might want the map to stay put while they scan. Worth prototyping whether selecting an island filters the list or zooms the map, or both. My instinct is both, but it's the kind of thing that only reveals itself in the hand.

What's settled, and what isn't

Settled

Two views, not three. Grid sells; Map decides. The list isn't a view — it's the map's left pane, sharing one zoom, so it is always “what's in view”. The destination dropdown is absorbed rather than replaced.

Everything here is powered by data that exists. Coordinates 16/16, amenities 15/16, Sabre IDs for the dated exit — all in docs/outrigger-properties.js.

Not settled

  • Mobile. Split-pane doesn't fit a phone and hover doesn't exist on touch. It becomes list-first with the map as a sheet, or a card carousel that pans the map — a second design, not a reflow. Your mobile share decides how much that matters, and I can't see it.
  • The synonym map. Those computed second lines only work once “Swimming pool” and “Outdoor pool & hot tub” collapse to one key. About twenty groups across the estate — an afternoon of judgement, and the last human work in the whole build.
  • Phi Phi Island Resort returned no amenities — different page template. One property to handle by hand.
