Outrigger · home page · version 2

The hero works. The page it opens onto doesn't.

An evaluation of Home Page Hero No Offer at 18 days, against the live page — and the change I'd make next, which is bigger than a hero.

What the test actually proved

12,804 per arm, 18 days, powered to ±18.2%

The mechanism is settled and it is strong. Hero CTA clicks −58.1%, p<0.0001. The offer no longer takes the first click — 286 clicks a fortnight that used to go to a discount page now go somewhere else.

And they genuinely go somewhere. Almost every one is accounted for:

Read the bottom half of that table carefully, because it is the whole finding. Guests open more destination tabs, use the dropdown more, and click into more properties. Then not one additional guest presses Book Now, opens an offer, or reaches the rates page. Every gain is a browse action. Every commerce action is flat or down.

Scroll depth is now flat-to-slightly-negative at all four levels (−0.3%, −0.7%, −0.9%, −2.6%). The new hero doesn't send people down the page. It sends them sideways, into the explorer and the menu.

The one genuinely good number: the guests who do get through are far better.

Bookings per arrival at Rooms & Rates went from 28.9% to 34.4% — about a fifth better. Fewer arrivals, materially more of them buying. The filter works. What's missing is volume through it.

Why — and it isn't the hero

I went and read the live page

The home page runs two economies that never touch.

There is an inspiration economy: the hero, the destination tabs, the property tiles, the Cirque promo, ten “Discover paradise” stories. Its exits are Learn more, Oahu highlights, Learn More, View more stories. Every one leads to more reading.

And there is a commerce economy: BOOK NOW in the header, the sticky BOOK NOW bar, Book Now on each tile, and five Check availability buttons in the offers block. Every one of those assumes you already know where and when you're going.

The old hero was a door between them: a price hook that dropped you straight into the commerce side. We removed that door and did not build another one. So the traffic we successfully redirected into inspiration now circulates there and leaves.

Version 2 — the change I'd make

Keep the hero. Give the page a door at every step.

Stop asking guests to declare a booking. Ask them a question instead.

Every commerce entry on this page demands a decision — where, when, book. Every inspiration exit offers only more reading. Version 2 puts one soft question in the hero and then lets its answer make every downstream surface priceable.

  1. Put a soft intent capture in the hero — island and month, not a date grid

    The hero currently spends its click on Learn more. Replace that with “Where, and roughly when?” — island chips and a month, no exact dates, no guest count. One tap and a month. That is a far lower commitment than the booking engine asks for, and it is enough to price anything.

    This is the load-bearing change. Everything below only works because the page now knows two things it currently never learns until the very end.

    Evidence: the hero's 286 displaced clicks prove attention is available at the top of the page. Nothing currently captures it.

  2. Make the destination tabs end in properties and rates, not “highlights”

    The tabs are the single biggest gainer (+8.3%, and the dropdown +12.7%) and their exit button is the one thing that went down (−7.0%). Guests are opening the explorer and refusing what it offers them. Replace “Oahu highlights” with the island's properties showing a from-rate for the chosen month.

    Evidence: +82 clicks into the tabs, −12 out of the highlights button.

  3. One action per property tile: “See rates”, replacing both “Book Now” and “Learn More”

    Tiles carry two competing CTAs and the guest picks neither — Book Now is flat (+1 click over eighteen days), Learn More is down 5.9%. Meanwhile the property name is up 11.3%. They want the place, not the brochure and not the commitment. “See rates” is the same destination as Book Now in a form somebody still choosing will actually press.

    Evidence: Title +11.3% against Book Now −0.7% and Learn More −5.9%.

  4. Cut “Discover paradise” from the home page

    Ten story links earning 0.46%, down 8.8%, sitting in a page where only a quarter of visitors reach the bottom. It is the largest block on the page by height and close to the smallest by return. Removing it moves the offers block — the most commerce-dense thing on the page, five “Check availability” buttons — meaningfully further up.

    Evidence: Story Clicked 0.50% → 0.46%; Scroll 100% only 24% and falling.

  5. Do not add anything below the fold

    Scroll is flat-to-negative at every depth. Any new inspirational content would be served to strictly the same eyeballs. This is the constraint that rules out the obvious follow-up, and it is the one thing in this test that is genuinely settled in the negative.

    Evidence: −0.3%, −0.7%, −0.9%, −2.6% across the four depths, composite −1.0%.

The tension, named

Putting a search-shaped thing in the hero is the most conventional move in hotel web design, and v1 was explicitly about escaping conventional discount-led framing. That tension is real and I'd resolve it this way: a rate for your dates is utility; a percentage off is a promise. Showing what a room costs in March is not the thing v1 removed. Leading with “40% off” is. Keep the imagery, keep the experience-led line, and let the question sit underneath it rather than replacing it.

The brief, ready to freeze

Home Page — From Looking to Rates

Hypothesis

We believe that capturing island and month in the hero, and giving the destination explorer and property tiles a direct rates entry that uses it, for home page visitors on the experience-led hero, will cause more arrivals at Rooms & Rates — because the new hero successfully creates browsing intent and the page currently offers that intent no route into a price.

Primary metric · expected to INCREASE

Visit Page: Booking Engine: Rooms & Rates — baseline 4.27%. The only step between proven intent and revenue, and the one thing v1 moved the wrong way.

Guardrails

  • Hero CTA Click — must stay down. A rebound means the door we opened is the old one.
  • Destination explorer engagement — the biggest v1 gain; must not be traded away
  • Property tile engagement — the other gain
  • Offer engagement — flat in v1; a fall means we've made the page transactional
  • Scroll depth engagement — already drifting down; must not accelerate
  • Bookings per arrival — 34.4% in v1. Volume must not come at the cost of quality.

Duration — computed

v1 accrued 12,804 per arm in 18 days (~711/arm/day). At a 4.27% baseline, a 20% relative move needs about 9,600 per arm — roughly 14 days. A 10% move would need ~38,000 per arm, about eight weeks. Commit to detecting 20%.

Watched, not decisive

Booking Complete (1.23%). Needs ~36,000 per arm to resolve 20% — about eight weeks. Pre-registering it as primary guarantees an unresolvable test.

One check before building.

The 28.9% → 34.4% quality finding assumes bookings pass through Visit Page: Booking Engine: Rooms & Rates. The page has at least four other routes into booking — header, sticky bar, tile Book Now, and five offer “Check availability” buttons. If a material share of bookings enters another way, that ratio compares two different populations and the primary metric may be measuring only part of the funnel. One analytics check settles it.

What I would not do

Not another hero copy test. −58.1% at p<0.0001 is as settled as this programme gets; running it again buys nothing. Not more home page content — scroll says it would not be seen. Not a return to offer-led framing — offer engagement was flat when the offer left the hero, which means offer demand is inelastic to the hero and the discount was never earning its position.
